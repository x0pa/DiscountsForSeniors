Best Western offers a senior rate to guests aged 55 and over, and its own offer page keeps the promise simple. The page is titled Senior Hotel Discounts, and its central line says that guests 55 and over save with discounted room rates at Best Western hotels. There is no percentage attached to that promise anywhere on the page, and that absence is the most important fact about this offer. The saving is whatever the hotel you choose takes off its room rate for your dates, and it can be modest on one night and worthwhile on another.

That starting age of 55 puts Best Western at the generous end of the hotel groups in this directory. Sandman also starts at 55, while IHG and Marriott wait until 62 and Hilton waits until 65. A traveller in their late fifties who gets no senior price at most big chains can still ask for one here. Best Western hotels sit in cities and small towns across Canada, often along the highways that road trips follow, so the rate turns up in exactly the places where a one night stop needs a fair price rather than a luxury splurge.

How to claim it

1. Search your destination and dates on the Best Western site or app and open the rates list in the search form.
2. Choose the AARP/Senior rate option before you search, since the results then show senior prices where a hotel offers them.
3. Compare the senior price with the standard price and any member price for the same room before you book.
4. Bring proof of your age, because the offer page warns in its own footnote that you will be asked to provide it when you arrive.

The lack of a published percentage has produced a crowd of invented numbers elsewhere. Pages that do not belong to Best Western variously promise 10 percent off, or savings of up to 15 percent, and they do not agree with each other because none of them comes from the chain itself. The offer page publishes no figure at all, so treat every percentage you read about this rate on another site as a guess. The only honest test is the one you run yourself: price the same room, on the same night, at the senior rate and at the regular rate, and see what the screen says.

The same page adds two smaller promises, and both deserve a careful read. It says guests can enjoy complimentary breakfast, yet its footnote adds that breakfast is not available at all Best Western hotels and is subject to availability at the time of request. In plain terms, some hotels include breakfast and some do not, and the hotel you pick decides. The page also notes that reward points never expire, which matters if you collect them on a senior stay today and spend them on a trip next year.

Because the discount is set hotel by hotel and night by night, the senior rate is not always the lowest price in the list. A sale price that asks for payment in advance can undercut it, and a member price can match it. The senior rate still earns its place in the comparison because it often keeps flexible cancellation terms while sale prices give those terms up. Weigh the total price and the cancellation terms together, not the headline alone.

Practical tip: run two searches for every stay, one with the AARP/Senior rate selected and one without it. The pair of results takes a minute to produce and tells you the true size of the saving for that hotel on that night, which is a number no other site can give you.
