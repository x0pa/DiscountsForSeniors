## Start with the fixed bills

Housing, utilities, phone and banking fees are the bills that show up every month, and several of them have senior pricing or senior account options. Canada's big banks all offer discounted accounts for customers 60+, and the difference can be a monthly fee that drops to zero.

## Stack the small savings

One discount day rarely changes a budget. A weekly pharmacy day, a grocery seniors day, senior transit fares and a bank account with no fees together can add up to real money over a year.

## Your government benefits

Pensions, benefits and credits are a separate topic, and an important one. Service Canada publishes the official details on CPP, OAS, GIS and provincial programs. For the same material walked through step by step in plain language, [EveryBenefit](https://everybenefit.ca/posts/turning-65-checklist/) lays out every benefit to sort out at 65, in the order to do them. Keep Service Canada as the official record; use the walkthrough to know what to look for and what to ask.
