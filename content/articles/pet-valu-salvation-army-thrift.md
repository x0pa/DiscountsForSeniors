Some savings hide because they are attached to errands people already run without thinking. The pet store run happens when the food bin looks low. The thrift store visit happens when you are nearby and curious. Pet Valu and the Salvation Army Thrift Store both offer shoppers aged 60 and older a clear 10% saving, yet the two offers work in opposite ways. One arrives on a single day each month and rewards planning. The other waits every day and rewards attention. Learn both, and two ordinary stops start paying a little of their own cost back.

## Pet Valu and the last Thursday

Pet Valu holds its Seniors Day on the last Thursday of every month. Shoppers aged 60 and older who belong to the Your Rewards loyalty programme save 10% on regular priced merchandise that day. The programme is free to join, and you can join in store or online before the day arrives. Joining ahead of time is the calm way to do it, because joining at a busy till, with a pet waiting in the car or a line forming behind you, turns a simple saving into a small ordeal.

Pet food is the big recurring cost in most pet households, and that is why this day is worth circling. Food is bought again and again, month after month, at a weight and a price that make 10% feel real by the time the bag reaches the counter. The whole trick is timing the monthly shop to that Thursday. Instead of buying food whenever the bin runs low, keep enough on hand to reach the last Thursday, then buy the month in one visit. Treats, toys and other regular priced merchandise for the pet can ride along in the same trip, bought on the day when they cost less.

A simple routine keeps the timing painless. Write the last Thursday on the calendar at the start of the month. Check the food bin a week ahead, so a low supply is a note on the list and not an emergency trip at full price. Keep the rewards membership details where you can find them, and bring ID, because the saving rests on your age as well as your membership. None of this takes more than a minute or two a month, and the minute is repaid every single time the big bag goes through the till.

## The Salvation Army Thrift Store and every day

The Salvation Army Thrift Store gives guests aged 60 and older 10% off every day at participating stores across Canada. There is no single date to circle and no membership to arrange. The saving is simply there, on clothing, household goods, books and whatever else the shelves hold that week, for any guest who qualifies and asks.

One rule shapes how to use it well. The discount cannot be combined with another offer, so on a store sale day, take whichever saving is larger. If a sale offers more than the senior discount on the item in your hand, take the sale. If the senior discount is the better figure, take that instead. You are not choosing a side. You are choosing the larger number, and a moment of arithmetic at the shelf is all it costs.

Thrift stock turns over constantly, and that changes what a standing discount means. A chain store shelf looks much the same next month, but a thrift store shelf next month holds a different coat, a different lamp and a different set of dishes. You cannot plan a thrift purchase the way you plan a pet food purchase. What you can do is visit with the discount in mind, look with patience and buy only when the right thing appears. The discount applies on top of thrift store prices, which are already low, and that is why it adds up to more than it sounds. Ten percent off a modest price is a modest sum once. Ten percent off a year of coats, kitchen goods, frames and furniture finds is a different story.

## Making both part of the routine

The two offers reward the same two habits, and the habits are worth naming plainly. Carry ID, and ask at the till. Staff cannot always guess your age, and many will not raise the subject themselves out of politeness. A short question settles it. At Pet Valu, ask before the order is rung through, with your rewards membership ready. At the thrift store, ask as your items are scanned, and mention if a sale sign has raised a question about which saving applies. Polite and early beats hopeful and late, every time.

The household still needs the coat, the pot or the chair. The only change is when you buy and whether you ask, and those two small changes, repeated through a year, are how the budget ends up eating as well as the dog does.
