Vancouver may be the best city in Canada to be a senior with a transit card. The concession fare follows you onto the bus, the train and the little ferry across the harbour, and four days a week the big ferries will carry you as a passenger for nothing at all. This guide covers the programmes Metro Vancouver seniors actually use, and every one of them sits in our [directory](/directory/?province=BC), filtered to British Columbia.

## How do seniors save on transit in Vancouver?

TransLink gives concession fares to seniors aged 65 and older across the bus network, the SkyTrain and the SeaBus. The concession price sits well below the adult fare: one zone travel on a Compass Card costs $2.30, and a concession monthly pass costs far less than the adult version.

Two habits make it work. First, pay with a Compass Card. The concession lives on the card, while paying cash or tapping a credit card charges the full adult fare. Second, carry photo ID that proves your age, because the concession is an age fare and staff can ask to see it. Set the card up once and the discount is silent from then on. Our [transit fares entry](/discounts/city-transit-senior-fares/) explains the pattern across Canadian cities.

## When do BC Ferries carry seniors for free?

British Columbia residents aged 65 and older travel free as passengers on BC Ferries from Monday to Thursday on most routes. Show your BC Services Card, or other proof of your age and your BC residency, when you buy the ticket.

Read the shape of that offer carefully, because it rewards planning. The free fare covers the passenger, not the car. If you drive aboard, the vehicle fare and the driver portion are handled the way the route prices them, so walk on passengers gain the most. Some routes, including the northern routes, treat the senior offer differently, so check your sailing before you count on it. The details are in our [BC Ferries entry](/discounts/bc-ferries-seniors/).

Used well, this is the single most valuable standing discount in the province: a midweek trip to Victoria, the Sunshine Coast or the Gulf Islands with the passenger fare removed entirely.

## Which Vancouver stores run weekly discount days?

Metro Vancouver runs on the same weekly rhythm as the rest of the country. Thursdays belong to Shoppers Drug Mart and its seniors day, with 20% off regular priced items for shoppers aged 65 and older. Wednesdays are the Bulk Barn seniors day for pantry staples. Tuesdays are the Value Village senior discount day, and the big stores on Grandview Highway and in Burnaby are worth the trip on that day in particular.

None of these need a special card beyond the loyalty card the store already uses. They need a calendar. The seniors who save the most in this city are simply the ones whose errands land on the right days. Our [directory filtered to British Columbia](/directory/?province=BC) collects all three days in one view, with the age and the terms for each programme, so the week plans itself at a glance.

## Days out

Cineplex senior pricing applies at theatres across Metro Vancouver all year. The major attractions publish senior admission as well: the Vancouver Aquarium in Stanley Park and Science World both price senior tickets below the adult rate, typically by a few dollars, and the gap widens when a senior rate meets a quiet weekday. Check the attraction's own ticket page before you go.

A good Vancouver day out stacks the pieces in order. Ride the SeaBus on the concession fare, use the senior admission at the attraction, and time the trip home before the evening crowds build. None of the individual savings feels dramatic at the till. Added together across a season of concerts, games, exhibitions and family visits, they are the difference between watching the budget and simply enjoying the city. That is the quiet promise of the programmes in this guide: small savings, repeated often, that you collect without thinking once the habits are set.

## Eating out

Denny's locations around Metro Vancouver run the 55+ menu every day, with smaller portions at smaller prices for guests aged 55 and older. It is the most dependable restaurant saving in the region precisely because it does not depend on a day of the week. Our [Denny's entry](/discounts/dennys-senior-menu/) has the details.

## The Vancouver habit

Put the concession on a Compass Card. Treat Monday to Thursday as ferry days. Let Thursday anchor the errands. Vancouver's discounts reward exactly that rhythm, and when a programme changes, we recheck it and update the listing. [Tell us](/contact/) if you spot one first.
