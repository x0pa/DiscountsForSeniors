Winnipeg seniors have a strong stack of discounts, and the city rewards people who learn its rhythm. A cheaper bus fare gets you downtown, a different store holds a discount day almost every weekday, and a beloved local diner chain keeps a menu just for guests 55 and older. This guide covers the programmes Winnipeggers actually use, and every national programme named here is listed in our [directory](/directory/?province=MB), filtered to Manitoba so you can check the details before you head out.

## How do seniors save on Winnipeg Transit?

Winnipeg Transit runs a senior fare for riders aged 65 and older, and you show photo ID when you board. The smart way to pay is a peggo card. On a peggo card the senior e cash fare is $1.55 a ride against $3.10 for the full fare, so the card cuts the ride in half. If you ride often, the senior monthly e pass costs $59.70 against $119.35 for the full fare pass, which makes a month of trips, appointments and visits remarkably reasonable. Paying cash costs more, at $2.95 for seniors against $3.45 full fare, so the card pays for itself in convenience as well as price. Children aged 11 and younger ride free with an adult who pays, which makes grandparent trips cheaper still: bring the grandchildren downtown and their rides cost nothing at all. These are the fares the city published for 2026, and Winnipeg adjusts fares most years, usually in January, so a quick check each winter keeps your budget honest.

## Which Winnipeg stores hold discount days?

Winnipeg runs on discount days, and the week almost plans itself once you know the pattern. Shoppers Drug Mart anchors Thursday, when its seniors day brings the biggest crowd of regulars to stores across the city. M&M Food Market runs its senior Tuesday, a handy day to stock the freezer with meals for busy weeks. Giant Tiger holds its seniors day on the first Monday of the month, so that one needs a circle on the calendar rather than a weekly habit. Bulk Barn takes Wednesday, perfect for baking supplies, spices, nuts and pantry staples bought by weight. Value Village takes Tuesday as well, for clothing, housewares and the occasional treasure. All of them have Winnipeg locations, most of them several, so whichever part of the city you call home, at least one store on this list sits within an easy bus ride or a short drive.

## What is the Salisbury House Silver menu?

Every city has a diner that feels like it belongs to everyone, and in Winnipeg that diner is Salisbury House. The chain was founded in 1931 and is famous for its Nips, the burgers Winnipeggers have argued about and loved for generations. What matters for this guide is its Silver menu, offered to guests aged 55 and older, with diner classics at smaller prices. Portions and prices suit a lighter appetite and a lighter bill, and the coffee refills and familiar rooms do the rest. Plenty of national chains offer a senior menu of some kind, but this one is ours, built in this city and still serving the neighbourhoods that grew up with it. If you have visitors in town, a Salisbury House stop explains Winnipeg better than most tours.

## Days out

Getting out of the house should not cost a fortune, and Winnipeg helps. Cineplex senior pricing applies at Winnipeg theatres, so a matinee with a friend costs less before you even think about the popcorn. The Manitoba Museum and the Winnipeg Art Gallery both publish senior admission below adult prices, which makes a slow morning with the galleries or the exhibits an easy yes rather than a special occasion. Both sit downtown, close to transit, and both reward visitors who come on quieter weekdays. That is the pattern to look for at attractions across the country, because many venues publish a senior rate quietly and wait for you to find it.

## Eating out

Beyond the Silver menu, the familiar national option is here too. The Denny's 55+ menu applies in Winnipeg as it does across the country, with smaller plates at smaller prices for guests aged 55 and older, served all day rather than in a narrow window. Our [Denny's entry](/discounts/dennys-senior-menu/) lays out how that menu works. Between Denny's, Salisbury House and the coffee counters around the city, a senior in Winnipeg can eat out most days of the week at a senior price of one kind or another. The common thread is simply knowing before you go, because none of these savings requires a coupon, a code or a special card beyond proof of age when asked.
