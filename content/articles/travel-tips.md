Travel is one of the best ways to stay active, meet new people and create lasting memories. With a little planning, Canadian seniors can enjoy rich experiences without overspending. Every rate named on this page comes from our directory, where each entry was checked against the operator own published policy. Here are ten tips to help you travel smarter and keep more money for the trip itself.

## 1. Price the senior hotel rate every time

Hotel senior rates start at very different ages, and that spread is worth money. Best Western and Sandman both start at 55. Sandman publishes savings of up to 30 percent at participating hotels, with a food and beverage gift card included in the offer. Best Western publishes discounted room rates for guests aged 55 and over, with no percentage attached, so the saving depends on the hotel and the night. Marriott and IHG both start at 62, and Hilton starts at 65. None of the large groups except Sandman publishes a single figure you can count on before you search. Price the same room at the senior rate and at the standard rate, on the same dates, and book the lower total. Bring proof of age, since hotels can ask to see it when you arrive. Our [hotel senior rates comparison](/articles/hotel-senior-rates-compared/) and the [directory](/directory/) hold the full terms for each chain.

## 2. Travel in the quiet season when you can

Spring and fall bring lower prices, smaller crowds and a calmer pace in most Canadian destinations. The calendar also holds savings that only appear outside the busy months. BC Parks gives BC residents aged 65 and up half the peak season base camping fee at frontcountry campgrounds during the off season window, which runs from the day after Labour Day through June 14. A September camping trip can pair mild weather and open trails with that lower fee. Where a discount has a season, plan the trip inside the season rather than hoping the rate will stretch to meet your dates.

## 3. Sort out insurance and health before you leave

Travel insurance gives peace of mind if plans change or an emergency interrupts a trip. Choose cover that fits the trip you are taking, and read what it says about health care, cancellations and delays before you pay. Pack medicines in the bag you keep with you, carry a list of what you take and when you take it, and leave room in each day for rest. A trip that protects your energy will feel better than a trip that packs every hour full.

## 4. Pack light and pack for comfort

Bring clothing that mixes well, shoes you have already walked in and only what you will truly use. A lighter bag is easier at stations, on ferries and in hotels with stairs. Keep documents, medicines and a phone charger in your carry bag, so a delayed suitcase cannot spoil the first day. Take a photo of your ID and your booking confirmations, and leave copies with family at home.

## 5. Set up your transit concession before travel day

City transit senior fares start at 65 on the systems in our directory, and each system runs its own rules. In Toronto, a senior fare on the TTC costs $2.30 in cash or $2.25 on PRESTO, and tapping a bank card charges the adult fare instead. GO Transit riders aged 65 and older save 55 percent off the GO fare with a PRESTO card. In Metro Vancouver, TransLink concession fares start at $2.30 for one zone, and a Compass Card is needed to receive the concession price. The lesson travels well across the country. Visit a service point with photo ID on a calm day, get the concession set on the right card and pay with that card on every trip.

## 6. Let parks and attractions anchor the trip

A Parks Canada Discovery Pass costs $71.50 for seniors aged 65 and older, against $83.50 for an adult, and it stays valid for 12 months at national parks, national historic sites and national marine conservation areas across the country. Daily admission also carries a senior price, at $10.75 against $12.25 for an adult. Add up your likely visits before you choose between the pass and daily fees. Attractions in our directory publish senior prices as well, from the CN Tower and the Toronto Zoo in Toronto to the Capilano Suspension Bridge near Vancouver and the Royal Tyrrell Museum in Alberta. Build a trip around two or three anchor visits, leave space between them and let the pass and the senior prices do quiet work on the budget.

## 7. Use ferries and regional travel on their best days

BC residents aged 65 and older ride free as passengers on many BC Ferries sailings from Monday to Thursday, excluding statutory holidays. The free fare covers the passenger fare only, and vehicle fares and other charges still apply. It also does not apply on the Inside Passage, Haida Gwaii and Central Coast Connector routes, where a separate senior passenger discount applies instead. In northern Ontario, Ontario Northland gives travellers aged 60 and over 15 percent off fares on its motor coach services, the Polar Bear Express and the Northlander. If your plans can move by a day or two, move them toward the days and routes where the saving lives.

## 8. Book direct and keep the terms in view

Senior hotel rates appear in the chain own booking path, and most other booking sites cannot sell them. Booking direct also makes it easier to see cancellation terms beside the price. A sale price that asks for payment in advance can beat a senior rate on the screen and still cost more if plans change. Compare the total price, the taxes and fees and the cancellation terms together. If a senior rate does not appear for your dates, the hotel may not take part or those dates may sit outside the offer, and a call to the hotel is the one move left.

## 9. Stay connected without fuss

Keep in touch with family and friends while you travel. Save hotel addresses, booking numbers and emergency contacts in your phone before you leave, and keep a paper copy in your bag. Check the weather for each stop, download maps while you have a good connection and share your rough plan with someone at home.

## 10. Enjoy the journey itself

The best trips are not a race through a list. Sit with a coffee and watch a harbour. Take the scenic road when time allows. Say yes to a local dish, a museum room you had not planned on or a conversation with a fellow traveller. Some of the best memories come from the moments nobody scheduled. Travel well, stay safe and make it count.
