Few questions land in our inbox more often than the coffee question. Does Tim Hortons give seniors a discount? Does McDonald's still sell a senior coffee? Is the coffee itself ever free? The answers online are confident, tidy and mostly wrong, because they describe a national policy that neither chain actually runs in Canada. We checked both chains against what the companies themselves say, the same standard we use for every listing in our [directory](/directory/). Neither chain appears in that directory, and this article explains why.

## The short answer

Neither chain runs a national senior discount programme in Canada. There is no Tim Hortons seniors day, no McDonald's senior price list that applies from coast to coast, and no age you reach that unlocks a set percentage at either till. That is not quite the end of the story, because both chains are built from individual restaurants, and individual restaurants sometimes do kind things for the regulars they know by name.

## Tim Hortons: no national policy, some local kindness

Tim Hortons itself says it does not offer a seniors discount all year round. Read that sentence carefully, because it leaves a door open. Its restaurants belong to individual franchise owners. Some owners choose to give older regulars a break, often on coffee. Others do not, and both choices sit within the rules. If your usual location quietly charges you less for a coffee, thank them and enjoy it. Just know that the location across town owes you nothing, because the kindness belongs to the owner, not to the chain.

## The 10 percent claim that will not die

Search for a Tim Hortons senior discount and you will meet the same claim again and again: 10% off for shoppers over 60. Coupon sites repeat it, list sites copy it from one another, and it sounds precise enough to be true. It is not a Tim Hortons policy. The company has not published it, and no national programme sits behind it. If your store honours that figure, that is the store making its own decision for its own customers. That is worth having. It is just not worth planning a trip around a location you have never visited, or arguing about at a counter where the staff have never heard of it.

## McDonald's: the senior coffee is real, and local

The famous senior coffee is real, but it is local. Many McDonald's restaurants sell a small coffee at a reduced price to customers aged 55 and older, and some discount other drinks as well. For a lot of Canadians, that reduced price coffee has been part of a morning routine for years, shared at the same table with the same friends. The catch is the word many. Many is not all, and the offer was never a published national promise you could point to on a menu board everywhere. It can be generous at one address and absent at the next.

## Why the answer changes from one street to the next

Nearly every restaurant in both chains is owned by a franchisee, a local business owner who hires the staff and decides how far local goodwill goes. That is why the age, the cup size and the price all vary by location at McDonald's. One restaurant may start the reduced price at 55, another at 60 or 65. One may apply it to a small coffee only, another to any size. Some locations have dropped the practice in recent years, often quietly, and others never had it at all. None of that is a staff member being difficult. It is a local decision, and the counter staff are usually just following the list their owner gave them.

## Free refills and other house rules

Free refills come up almost as often as the coffee price itself. Where refills exist, they are a house practice at some locations rather than a published promise from either company. A dining room that refills your cup while you sit with friends is offering hospitality, not honouring a policy you can demand at another location. Enjoy it where it happens, and do not budget around it.

## The savings that are actually guaranteed

The savings that are guaranteed at both chains come from the apps, and they are open to everyone. The McDonald's app carries rotating offers that often include coffee and breakfast items, and a regular visitor can stack real savings over a month simply by checking the offers before ordering. At Tim Hortons, Tims Rewards turns every visit into points, and points turn into coffee and baked goods. For a daily coffee drinker, those points add up quickly. Neither programme asks your age. That is precisely why they work: they are published, they are national, and they apply at the counter every time once you have signed up.

## How to ask, and what to do with the answer

Our advice is simple, and it matches what we say in our guide to [stores with no senior discount](/articles/no-senior-discount/). Ask once at the location you actually visit, because that answer is the only one that matters. Ask with a smile, take the answer as final for that store, and remember it for next time. If the answer is yes, you have found a small daily pleasure at a better price. If the answer is no, you still have the app offers and the points, which are worth more over a year than most people guess. Do not quote a coupon site at the counter. The staff did not write it, and they cannot honour a policy their owner never made.
