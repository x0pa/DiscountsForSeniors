Staff rarely volunteer a senior discount. Sometimes they do not know their own store runs one: discount days live in head office documents, not in till training. Asking, the right way, is a skill worth having, and it pays out for the rest of your life.

## The words that work

1. **Ask before you pay.** "Do you have a senior discount today?", asked as the first items are scanned, not after the receipt prints. Applying a discount after payment often means a refund and buying it all again, a dance nobody enjoys.
2. **Name the day if there is one.** "It's Thursday. Is the seniors day on?" A specific question gets a specific answer, and it tells the cashier exactly where to look in their own system.
3. **Have ID ready.** Photo ID with your birth date settles every argument about whether you qualify. Offer it before you're asked; it turns the whole exchange into a formality.
4. **Ask at booking, not at checkout.** Hotels, attractions and rail tickets price differently at reservation time. "Do you have a senior rate?" belongs in the booking call or the online checkout's rate options. By the front desk, the price is usually already set.
5. **Ask the person who can say yes.** Staff at the front counter work from what's on their screen. For anything unusual (a big purchase, a group booking, a long stay), the manager or the bookings line has discretion the till does not.

> The discount exists whether or not the person at the till has heard of it. Be kind, be specific, and ask for the policy.

## If they say no

Stay friendly, and be precise. "It's listed on your company's website. Could we check together?" solves most cases; head office policies genuinely do not always reach the store. If the answer is "participating locations only," asking whether this location participates is fair game: sometimes the store has simply never enrolled, and your question is the first nudge.

If the programme really has ended, that's useful news too: [tell us](/contact/) what happened, with the store and date. We check it again against the retailer's published policy the same day, and if it has changed, the listing is updated or marked ended, so the next reader hears the new answer at the till instead of the old one.

## Three habits worth building

- **Keep one card ready.** The ID you reach for most should live in the front of your wallet, not the bottom of a bag.
- **Keep the list short.** You don't need every discount in Canada memorised. You need the ones on your weekly route. The [directory](/directory/) filters them by your age and province in seconds.
- **Ask once per new place.** Every new café, shop or service gets the question exactly once. A yes compounds for years; a no costs you five seconds.

That is the whole method: specific words, early in the transaction, with ID in hand, and a polite policy check when the answer surprises you.
