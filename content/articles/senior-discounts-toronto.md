Toronto seniors have a solid stack of discounts. The trick is knowing which ones run automatically and which ones you have to ask for. This guide covers the programmes Torontonians actually use, from transit and weekly discount days to the two attractions readers ask about most, the Toronto Zoo and the CN Tower. Every programme named here is listed in our [directory](/directory/?province=ON), filtered to Ontario, and each price below comes from the entry for that programme, checked against the operator own published policy.

## How do seniors save on transit in Toronto?

The TTC senior fare touches almost every trip. For riders aged 65 and older, a senior fare costs $2.30 in cash or $2.25 on PRESTO. The concession is not automatic. Take photo ID to a service point once, have the senior fare set on your PRESTO card, and from then on the discount is permanent. Pay with that card every time. Tapping a bank card charges the adult fare of $3.30, with no senior discount at all.

Monthly fare capping adds a second layer. Senior Monthly and 12 Month Passes were discontinued on August 31, 2026, when capping began. Under capping, 47 eligible fares in a calendar month are charged, and rides after that are free. Cash fares and PRESTO Tickets do not count toward capping, so the cap rewards riders who pay by PRESTO card. For trips beyond the city, GO Transit riders aged 65 and older save 55% off the GO fare with a PRESTO card. Details are in our [transit fares entry](/discounts/city-transit-senior-fares/).

## Which Toronto stores run weekly discount days?

Three days carry most of the value. Tuesdays are senior day at Value Village. Shoppers aged 60 and older save 30% on their purchases, and the saving excludes new merchandise, which is the stock wearing red tags in the store. Wednesdays are seniors day at Bulk Barn for shoppers aged 65 and older. Bulk Barn does not publish the percentage, so ask staff what the discount covers on that day.

Thursdays are the anchor. Shoppers Drug Mart seniors day gives shoppers aged 65 and older 20% off regular priced merchandise when they use their own PC Optimum card or app. The words regular priced matter. Sale items may not be reduced by the same amount, so check the shelf price as you shop and confirm the saving before you pay. Keep a running list during the week, do the pantry shop on Wednesday, and do the pharmacy shop on Thursday.

## What does a senior pay at the Toronto Zoo?

The Toronto Zoo sells a Senior ticket to every guest aged 65 and older, and the zoo publishes its full price list on its own site. A senior pays $38 in peak season against $45 for an adult, and $30.50 in the off season against $35, so the saving holds all year. Peak season runs from May 2 to October 31, and the off season runs from November 1 to May 1. All prices are plus tax. Child tickets cover ages 3 to 12 and cost $32 in peak season and $25 in the off season.

Buy the Senior ticket on the zoo website or at the gate, and bring photo ID in case staff ask at entry. If you visit more than once or twice a year, look at membership as well. The zoo publishes Senior memberships and Dual Senior memberships beside its daily rates, and a membership can cost less than a few days of admission. Weekday mornings in the off season are the calmest time to visit, and your ticket costs the least then as well. See the full terms in our [Toronto Zoo entry](/discounts/toronto-zoo-senior-tickets/).

## What does a senior pay at the CN Tower?

The CN Tower publishes a Senior price on every ticket it sells, for guests aged 65 and older. The most common choice is Timed General Admission, at $47 for an adult and $34 for a senior. The Top plus Timed General Admission costs $59 and $46. A Flex ticket costs $62 and $49. Sea the Sky, which adds entry to Ripley's Aquarium and is sold online only, costs $79 and $54.25. Premier costs $99 and $76, and the VIP Tour costs $199 and $176. Children aged 2 and under enter free, and taxes are extra on all prices.

The saving grows with the ticket. It is $13 on Timed General Admission and $24.75 on Sea the Sky, so the richer tickets carry the bigger senior saving. Book the Senior ticket on the tower site and book ahead if you can. Tickets can be booked up to 30 days in advance. Bring photo ID in case staff ask when you arrive, and compare the Flex ticket before you buy if your plans might shift. See the full list in our [CN Tower entry](/discounts/cn-tower-senior-tickets/).

## Where else do Toronto seniors save on days out?

Cineplex sells a Senior ticket category for guests aged 65 and older at Toronto theatres all year. Cineplex publishes no national senior price, so each theatre sets its own, and the price in front of you at booking is the one that counts. On Tuesdays the senior and general prices are often the same, because Tuesday pricing applies to everyone.

The best kept secret is still the library. A Toronto Public Library card unlocks the Museum + Arts Pass, which lends free admission to major Toronto attractions. It is not an age discount at all, since it is available to every cardholder, but seniors who use it in rotation save more on culture than with any other single programme in this guide.

## Eating out and everyday savings

Mandarin offers guests aged 65 and older 20% off the buffet price with photo ID, all year, at its Ontario restaurants. Tell your server before you settle the bill. Denny's locations in the Toronto area run the 55+ menu every day at participating restaurants, with smaller portions at lower prices. Ask for that menu as soon as you sit down.

For groceries, note the honest answer on Sobeys and Safeway. Neither banner publishes a chain wide seniors day, and no age, percentage, or fixed day exists for the chain as a whole. Individual stores sometimes run their own day, and a local day would appear in the local flyer and nowhere else. That is why our [Sobeys entry](/discounts/sobeys-safeway-seniors-days/) says to ask your own store rather than promise a day.

## The Toronto habit

Set the PRESTO concession once. Anchor Thursday. Put the Zoo and the CN Tower senior prices in the plan before you buy any ticket, and keep the library pass in rotation. Prices and terms can change, so check the attraction own ticket page before a special trip. When a programme changes, we check it again and update the listing.
