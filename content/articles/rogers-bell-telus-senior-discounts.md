Phone bills are one of the bills people most hope their age will shrink. The question comes up in stores and on phone lines every day, and it is a fair question. Canadians aged 65 and older often live on incomes that stay fixed, and a phone is not a luxury.

So we checked what Rogers, Bell and Telus actually publish about discounts for older customers. The honest answer is less tidy than the headlines on other sites suggest. There is one partial exception worth knowing about, two clear answers of no, and a few moves that lower a phone bill far more reliably than any discount based on age. Our [directory](/directory/) lists savings we can verify, and this article follows the same rule.

## The short answer

No Canadian carrier takes a percentage off your existing bill just because of your age. There is no standing senior discount on regular plans at any of the big three carriers. If you hold a regular plan at Rogers, Bell or Telus today, turning 65 does not change its price, and neither does asking a store clerk to apply a senior rate.

Rogers is the partial exception, and the next section explains its conditions in full. Bell offers no senior discount at all. Telus publishes no discount based on age on its regular plans, though it does run a separate programme priced for people on low incomes.

## Does Rogers offer senior plans?

Rogers publishes dedicated plans for customers aged 65 and older. Alongside those plans, Rogers offers a small extra monthly senior discount on the first line. That sounds simple, and it is real, but the conditions matter a great deal.

The plans for customers aged 65 and older are for new activations only. You bring your own phone, rather than financing a new one through Rogers as part of the offer. Government issued photo ID is required, so the age on the account gets proven at the start. The offer covers up to two lines, which helps couples who share an account and stops there.

That list changes who can use the offer. This is a separate set of plans, not a discount applied to a plan you already hold. If you are a longtime Rogers customer on a regular plan, you cannot simply have the senior discount added to that plan. You would be looking at moving to the new plans as a new activation, with your own phone, and deciding whether the change is worth it. For some people it will be. For others, the plan they already hold, or a cheaper brand below, will cost less.

## Does Bell offer a senior discount?

Bell does not offer a senior discount. That answer appears plainly in Bell's own community forum, where the question has been asked and answered in public. Nothing on Bell's plan pages shows a senior offer either. What those pages show is promotions open to everyone, such as seasonal offers and deals tied to new phones or new lines.

That distinction matters at the store counter. A promotion open to everyone may still be a good deal. But it is not a senior discount, and it will not last forever. If a Bell offer looks attractive, ask how long the price lasts, what it becomes when the promotion ends, and whether a plan from one of the discount brands below would cost less every month.

## Does Telus offer a senior discount?

Telus publishes no discount based on age on its regular plans. There is no senior rate to ask a Telus store to apply, and no percentage that comes off a regular bill at 65.

Telus does run a programme priced for people on low incomes. Some seniors reach that programme through income rather than age, because eligibility rests on income and not on a birthday. If your income is modest, it is worth asking Telus directly what that programme involves and what proof it asks for, and taking the details from Telus itself rather than from a site summarizing it at a distance.

## What actually lowers a bill

Start with a phone call. Ask the carrier, in plain words, for the cheapest plan for someone aged 65 and older. At Rogers, that question leads to the plans described above.

Next, look at the discount brands. Each big carrier owns brands that sell simpler plans at lower prices, often using the same network. At Rogers, those brands are Fido and Chatr. At Bell, look at Virgin Plus and Lucky Mobile. At Telus, look at Koodo and Public Mobile. The monthly saving is real, and it repeats every month.

Bring your own phone whenever you can. A phone that still works well is money already spent, and plans built around your own phone are usually the cheapest plans a carrier sells. Finally, drop data you never use. Check your last few bills, see what you actually used, and choose a plan that fits that pattern with a little room to spare.

## A warning about hidden rates

Search online and you will find third party sites promising hidden senior rates at all three carriers, sometimes with exact percentages and confident instructions for claiming them. The carriers themselves do not publish those rates. A percentage printed on a site that sells nothing and answers to no one is not a policy.

Trust the carrier's own plan pages, its own forum answers, and what its staff will put in writing. If a rate is real, it can be shown to you on an official page or on a bill. If it cannot, shop the brands and make the call.
