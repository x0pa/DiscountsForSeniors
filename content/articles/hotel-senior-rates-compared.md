Ask three hotel chains what a senior rate means and you get three different answers. The starting age changes, the size of the saving changes, and one of the three does not publish an age based rate in the first place. What never changes is the catch: a senior rate is one rate among several, and it is not always the cheapest one on the screen.

This comparison covers the three groups Canadian travellers meet most often. Each rate below was checked against the chain's own published terms, the same standard we apply to everything in our [directory](/directory/). The directory holds the short versions; this page is the detail.

## Marriott: starts at 62, sized by the hotel

Marriott offers a senior rate to guests aged 62 and older at participating hotels across its brands, which run from Fairfield and Courtyard up to Sheraton, Westin and the Marriott hotels themselves.

Marriott does not publish one fixed percentage. The rate is set property by property and date by date. On the right night it sits clearly below the standard flexible rate. On the wrong night it can match the regular price exactly, which is why the comparison habit matters more here than any headline number.

To book it, choose the senior rate option when you search on the Marriott site or app. One reservation at the senior rate can cover up to two rooms, and the front desk can ask for photo ID when you arrive, so the guest named on the booking should be the one who qualifies.

## Hilton: starts at 65, sized by the hotel

Hilton sets the highest bar of the three. Its senior rate is for guests aged 65 and older at participating hotels across brands such as Hilton, DoubleTree, Hampton and Embassy Suites, and the size of the discount varies by hotel.

The saving is modest on most nights, and Hilton knows it: the senior rate often lands very close to the Hilton Honors member rate, which costs nothing to join. On many nights the two prices are within a dollar or two of each other, and sometimes the member rate wins.

To find it, open the Special Rates section of the booking form and select Senior Rate. The limit is two rooms per reservation, and you should expect to show ID at check in. If you travel with Hilton often, price the senior rate and the member rate side by side every single time.

## Choice Hotels: no age based rate we could verify

Choice is the odd one out, and it earns its place in this comparison for exactly that reason. We checked the terms Choice itself publishes and found no age based senior rate there. The rate that directories describe, for guests aged 60 and older who book in advance, does not appear in anything Choice itself publishes, so we will not present it as a fact.

What Choice does publish is a saving of 10% for AARP members at participating hotels. AARP is a paid membership, and it is open to adults of any age, starting at 18. If you hold that membership, the saving is real, and it suits road trips, because the Choice brand list is the road trip list: Comfort, Quality, Sleep Inn, Clarion, Econo Lodge and Rodeway Inn, plus the Ascend collection. In much of Canada these are the hotels in the smaller cities and along the highways, which makes the member rate the one most likely to matter on a driving holiday.

If you do not hold the membership, there is no verified senior rate at Choice for you to claim. Compare the public sale rates instead, and treat any site that promises you a Choice discount based on age as a site that has not done the checking.

## The rules that apply at all three

**The name on the reservation carries the qualification.** For a senior rate, the guest who books must meet the age threshold. For the Choice AARP rate, the guest who books must hold the membership. A spouse, a friend or an adult child travelling in the same room does not have to qualify, but the qualifying guest does have to be the one who reserved.

**A senior rate is a category, not a guarantee.** Every chain on this page also sells member rates, advance purchase rates and seasonal sales. Any one of them can undercut the senior rate on a given night. The senior rate takes thirty seconds to check, and checking it is the entire skill.

**Book direct to see it.** The senior rate appears in the chain's own booking path. Most third party booking sites cannot sell it, and a room booked through them cannot usually be changed to the senior rate later.

**Participating is doing quiet work in every sentence.** Not every hotel in a chain takes part, and a hotel that offers the rate in February may not offer it during a festival weekend in July. If the rate does not appear for your dates, it is not available for those dates, and calling the hotel directly is the one remaining move.

## The bottom line

At 62, Marriott is the first door of the three to open, with the widest spread between a good night and a bad one. At 65, Hilton joins it, with a saving that changes from hotel to hotel. Choice opens its door only through AARP membership, which has nothing to do with age. None of them rewards loyalty to the rate itself. All of them reward the traveller who prices the room twice.
