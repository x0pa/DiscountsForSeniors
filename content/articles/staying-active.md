Ask Canadians who are thriving in their seventies what changed after 60, and few of them mention a gym membership. They talk about routines: the walking group that meets rain or shine, the Tuesday swim, the garden that needs them every morning. Staying active is less about exercise than about having somewhere to be, and most of the best options cost very little.

## Movement that fits a budget

1. **Walking is the whole programme, if you let it be.** A daily walk covers more of what doctors recommend than any machine. Make it social and it sticks: mall walking groups meet before stores open in hundreds of Canadian malls, and municipal trails groups run free guided walks in most cities.
2. **Check your community centre's senior rate.** Most city recreation centres discount memberships and drop in fees at 55 or 65, often 25% to 35% off the adult price, and sometimes free for seniors on a low income through municipal subsidy programmes. The senior rate is rarely on the poster; ask at the front desk.
3. **Try the pool at lane swim time.** Swimming is kind to joints and brutal on boredom. Many pools run dedicated 55+ swim times at the senior drop in rate, with the added bonus of a regular crowd.
4. **Pickleball earns its hype.** It is cheap (a paddle and a drop in fee), genuinely social, and scaled for real bodies. There is a reason it is the fastest growing sport among Canadian seniors. Most recreation centres run beginner sessions.

## Active doesn't have to mean exercise

5. **Gardening counts, fully.** Digging, carrying, squatting, walking the rows, a working garden is a routine for the whole body disguised as a hobby, and it pays you back in tomatoes.
6. **Volunteering keeps you moving and needed.** Food banks, museums, hospitals, charity shops and school reading programmes all run on senior volunteers, and the shifts involve more walking and lifting than you'd expect. Local attractions often recruit volunteer guides, with free admission as a perk.

> The goal is not to exercise more. It is to build a week that happens to contain movement, people and fresh air, and then let the week carry you.

## Make the trip part of it

7. **Use your senior transit fare to get there.** At roughly half the adult fare in most big cities, the bus or train stops being a cost barrier to the pool, the class or the walking group. See our [transit fares entry](/discounts/city-transit-senior-fares/) for how the concession works.
8. **Anchor activity to an existing discount day.** Swim on the morning of your pharmacy discount day, walk the mall on thrift store Tuesday. Habits that share a day reinforce each other, and our [week at a glance](/articles/discount-week-at-a-glance/) shows how the days line up.

Start with one anchor: one class, one group, one regular walk with a regular person. After 60, the best fitness programme is the one still running next year, and that is always the one you actually enjoy.
