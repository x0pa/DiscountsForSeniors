The discounts on big ticket items get the attention: the hotel rate, the cruise fare. But the money most of us actually save as seniors comes from the boring, weekly spending: groceries, prescriptions, soap and light bulbs. Get the everyday essentials right and the savings repeat 52 weeks a year. Here is how to work the system.

## Start with the calendar, not the cart

1. **Shop on your discount day.** Most grocery and pharmacy savings for seniors run one day a week. Shoppers Drug Mart's seniors day (65+, Thursdays) takes 20% off items at the regular price, and Bulk Barn's seniors day (65+, Wednesdays) covers the pantry staples. Moving your main shop to the right day is worth more than a month of coupon clipping.
2. **Stack the discount day with the flyer.** A senior discount usually applies to regular prices, so the real skill is choosing which items to buy on discount day (the ones at the regular price) and which to buy from the flyer any day of the week. Two smaller trips often beat one big one.
3. **Keep a running list by store.** When something you use is cheap somewhere, note where. Toothpaste at one store, laundry detergent at another. After a month, the list shops itself.

## At the pharmacy counter

4. **Ask about the house brand.** Store brand vitamins, pain relievers and cold remedies are typically made to the same standards as the name brands on the next shelf, for noticeably less. Your pharmacist can confirm the active ingredients match.
5. **Ask whether a larger size or a 90-day supply costs less per dose.** For medications you take every day, the difference in price per unit adds up quietly. One question at the counter, savings all year.

> Nobody gets rich from one discount day. The win is a routine: the right day, the right store, the same short list of questions at every till.

## The bills you forget you are paying

6. **Read your bank statement once a year.** If you are over 60 and still paying a monthly chequing fee, you are probably owed a switch: every Big Five bank has a senior account that reduces or waives it. Banks rarely move you automatically. You have to ask. Our [directory](/directory/) has a separate entry for each of the five banks.
7. **Call one provider a year and ask for the retention offer.** Phone, internet and insurance companies all keep quieter prices for customers who ask politely and mention they are comparing. Twenty minutes on the phone is regularly worth $20 to $40 a month.
8. **Check the small subscriptions.** Streaming services, apps and delivery memberships renew silently. If you have not used it this month, cancel it. You can almost always rejoin later at the same price.

## Free counts too

9. **Your library card is a discount card.** Canadian libraries lend far more than books: many offer free passes to museums and attractions, free streaming, and free courses. It is the one card that never charges you for using it.
10. **Ask, everywhere, every time.** The hardware store, the garden centre, the local café. Many independent businesses offer a senior price that is written down nowhere. The full habit is in our guide to [asking for a senior discount](/articles/how-to-ask/).

None of this requires giving anything up. It is the same groceries, the same prescriptions, the same bills, bought on the right day, at the right store, with two or three questions asked at the right moment.
