A senior discount nobody can actually get is worse than no listing at all. So we work to a standard, and this page is the standard, in public.

## The rules

1. **The retailer's own words.** A discount only gets listed when we can point to the retailer's own published policy: their website, program terms, or official price list. A forum post or another blog is a lead, never a source.
2. **The fine print is the listing.** Who qualifies (the exact age), what the discount is, when it runs (many are one day a week), and where it applies. If a programme is regional or run by a franchise, the listing says so.
3. **Every listing carries a date.** The "last verified" stamp on each entry is the day we last checked it against the source. No stamp, no listing.
4. **We check again on a cycle.** Programmes change quietly. The whole directory gets another pass on a regular cycle, and reader reports jump the queue.
5. **Dead programmes get marked, not buried.** When a discount ends, the listing says it ended and when. A site that hides its dead listings cannot be trusted about its live ones.
6. **Honest "no" answers.** Some famous chains have no senior discount at all (Costco, Walmart Canada, Starbucks and Home Depot among them). We publish those answers too, in our [article busting the myths](/articles/no-senior-discount/), because a directory that only says "yes" is an ad.

## If a store says no

Show the listing, ask politely for the manager, and check the retailer's policy page together. Then [tell us what happened](/contact/). If the programme has changed, we update the stamp or mark it ended, and the next reader is not embarrassed at the till.

*Government pensions, benefits and tax credits are not covered here. This site lists commercial discounts only.*
